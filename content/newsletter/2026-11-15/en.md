## Today’s focus

### 01 · CLIMATE · COP31: funding and targets remain disputed {#item-01}

On Monday 16 November the second and final week of COP31 begins: the UN’s annual climate conference, meeting in Antalya, Türkiye, from 9 to 20 November. The first week fits into the headline Climate Home News ran on Saturday: “limited progress”. Negotiators have taken the texts as far as they can without political decisions; now ministers are arriving to make them.

**The idea**

A COP decides by consensus: a text is adopted only if no country objects. Hence the weight of the “cover decision”, the political statement that sums up what the conference achieved. This year power is shared: Türkiye presides over the COP (Murat Kurum, its environment minister) and Australia presides over the negotiations (Chris Bowen), an arrangement reached to settle a dispute over hosting, Hürriyet Daily News recalls. According to Climate Home News, some large developing countries have spent the week objecting to the very idea of a cover decision, and above all to any sectoral targets. On the table is a voluntary pledge championed by Türkiye: raising electricity’s share of global final energy use to 35% by 2035, from 23% today according to Climate Home News (about 20% according to Hürriyet). For the EU (European Union), Wopke Hoekstra signalled it may drop its demand for “clean” electrification: “a certain degree of pragmatism is probably warranted and needed,” he said. African countries, for their part, want adaptation finance tripled to at least 120 billion dollars a year by 2035.

**Key dates**

- **9 November** · COP31 opens in Antalya.
- **14 November** · Midway stocktaking plenary, co-chaired by Türkiye and Australia.
- **16 November** · The final week begins; a first draft of the cover decision is expected early in the week, according to Climate Home News.
- **20 November** · Last scheduled day on the calendar of the UNFCCC (the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change).

**Did you know?**

According to Chris Bowen, quoted by Hürriyet, delegates have 160 agenda items and 60 substantive mandates to get through.

### 02 · CZECH REPUBLIC · Prague rallies for its public broadcasters {#item-02}

On Sunday 15 November a huge crowd gathered on Letná plain in Prague against a plan by Andrej Babiš’s government to replace the licence fee for public television and radio with funding from the state budget. Its size is disputed: “tens of thousands” according to Al Jazeera, 350,000 to 400,000 according to media estimates cited by Euronews. The fee currently costs 205 crowns a month per household. That is the critics’ whole argument: a broadcaster paid directly by its audience would depend less on those in power than one whose budget is voted every year. The government says editorial independence will not be affected; the EBU (European Broadcasting Union) and Reporters Without Borders, among others, criticise the reform, which has already passed a first reading.

### 03 · PUBLIC SECTOR · A rise for France’s lowest-paid civil servants, mostly thanks to the minimum wage {#item-03}

Speaking on franceinfo on Sunday 15 November, David Amiel, France’s minister for public action and accounts, confirmed “a rise in points for the lowest salaries in the civil service”. In all, 1.35 million public employees will get a pay rise, worth about 350 euros gross a year on average, at a cost of “a little over a billion euros”. The breakdown matters: 1.1 million of them owe it to the rise in the Smic (the minimum wage) announced on Monday 9 November. On Friday 13 November, Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu opened a month of talks with the unions on low pay; the same day, the CFDT union joined the strike call, saying the proposals “do not amount to real progress”.

### 04 · CYBERSECURITY · Contact details of 22 million people stolen from a La Poste subsidiary {#item-04}

La Poste announced on Saturday 14 November that Maileva, its business mail platform, was hit by a cyber-attack in mid-September: the names and postal addresses of about 22 million people, recipients of letters sent by its clients, were stolen, according to the group. Bank details are not affected, according to Maileva. The Paris prosecutor’s office confirmed on Sunday that the cybercrime brigade is investigating. Why it matters: the victims are not Maileva’s customers but those of a social landlord or a hospital that used it. Foch hospital in Suresnes warned patients that payment reminders “may have been read by a hacker”. The main risk, according to an expert quoted by franceinfo, is fake letters. If a request looks unusual, contact the sender through another channel.

### 05 · MIGRATION · A record day in the Canaries {#item-05}

According to an updated provisional tally published on Sunday 15 November by Spain’s EFE news agency, 1,580 people arrived in the Canary Islands, the Spanish archipelago off West Africa, on Saturday aboard fifteen boats, including 1,327 at El Hierro. An earlier tally, reported by Euronews, counted 1,488; a late arrival carrying another 92 people accounts for the difference. The previous daily record was 1,461, set on 7 November 2020. El Hierro, which has taken in more than 8,000 people in five weeks, has declared a social emergency. The route runs more than 1,000 kilometres from Senegal, Gambia and Mauritania, and it kills: the IOM (International Organization for Migration) recorded at least 450 deaths on it in the first ten months of 2026, according to Al Jazeera.

## Explainer

### 06 · El Niño {#item-06}

**Why it's in the news.** On 12 November the Climate Prediction Center of NOAA (the US ocean and atmosphere agency) put the surface temperature anomaly at +2.5°C in the Pacific’s reference zone, known as Niño-3.4. It estimates a better than 79% chance of a strong to very strong event through January–March 2027. The chance of a record since 1950 is about 60%, according to a three-month relative index: it cannot be inferred from the current +2.5°C reading alone.

**What it is.** El Niño is the warm phase of a natural oscillation in the tropical Pacific. Normally the trade winds blow from east to west along the equator. When they weaken, the ocean surface warms across the central and eastern Pacific, and rainfall shifts: less over Indonesia, more over the central Pacific. According to the WMO (World Meteorological Organization), an event comes round every two to seven years, lasts nine to twelve months and peaks between November and February. It is not caused by climate change, but it adds to it: its effect on global temperature is strongest in the year after it develops. For example, +2.5°C means that this band of ocean is 2.5°C warmer than normal.

### 07 · Banning the phone is not the same as teaching it {#item-07}

Since 1 September 2026, phones and connected devices have been banned in French lycées (upper secondary schools), breaks included. In The Conversation (in French), Anne Cordier, professor of information and communication sciences at the University of Lorraine, takes the measure seriously without settling for it: “putting a phone out of reach does not teach you how to use it”. She points out that platforms are designed to capture attention, so self-control alone cannot be the answer, and suggests involving pupils in writing the rules. Why click: a piece that refuses both panic and laissez-faire.
