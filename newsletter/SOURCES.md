# Sources

The public catalogue the editor starts from. These feeds and publications
surface **candidates**; they are not the edition. Once a subject is
shortlisted, research goes wherever the evidence is, primary sources first
(see [`EDITORIAL.md`](./EDITORIAL.md)).

Every feed URL below was checked to return a valid RSS or Atom document on
2026-11-14. A feed that stops resolving is removed or replaced in the same
change that notices it, never left to fail quietly.

**Access** says what a reader gets:

- **open**: free to read;
- **partial**: some articles free, some behind a paywall or meter;
- **discovery only**: mostly paywalled. Use it to notice a subject, then
  source the item from an open or primary source where one exists. Cite it
  only when the article is reachable and its key facts are stated in the item.

## Primary sources

Not feeds, but where research should land whenever they hold the original:

- courts and regulators: the decision or ruling itself (Conseil d'État, Cour
  de cassation, UK Supreme Court, CJEU, ECHR, competition authorities);
- parliaments and governments: the bill, the vote record, the official
  release (Légifrance, legislation.gov.uk, EUR-Lex, gov.uk, gouvernement.fr);
- statistics offices and central banks: INSEE, ONS, Eurostat, the ECB, the
  Bank of England, the IMF, the OECD, the World Bank;
- international bodies: UN agencies, the IPCC, the WHO;
- research: the paper or preprint (journal site, arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv),
  with its DOI when it has one;
- companies and projects: the filing, the changelog, the repository, the
  official announcement.

## Changing this catalogue

Add a source when it has repeatedly surfaced subjects the rest missed; remove
one that has stopped earning its place or stopped resolving. Check the feed
URL resolves before committing, and keep the sections balanced: the catalogue
should not tilt the edition towards one region, one language or one subject.
