## Today’s focus

### 01 · Why water costs more in Port-Lune {#item-01}

In the imaginary city of Port-Lune, the water bill has risen by a third in two years, according to the annual report of the régie, the public body that runs the network. Three causes overlap: pipes laid sixty years ago, a falling water table and a variable-rate loan.

**The idea**

A public utility charges for water at cost: when leaks grow, it has to produce more to deliver the same amount, and the customer pays the difference.

**Key dates**

- **1966** — The network goes in\
  The city lays most of its cast-iron pipes, designed to last fifty years.
- **2019** — The loan\
  The utility borrows at a variable rate to rebuild its treatment works.
- **2024** — The first rise\
  The price per cubic metre goes up 14%, the first increase in ten years.

**Did you know?**

According to the city’s open data, 28% of the water produced never reaches a tap.

**Further reading**

The council votes in January on spreading the works out or raising prices again.

## Explainer

### 05 · What is a review clause? {#item-05}

**Why it’s in the news.** The Kestrel Strait fisheries accord contains one.

**What it is.** A provision that lets the parties reopen an agreement before it expires if a condition set in advance is met, here the recovery of herring stocks.
